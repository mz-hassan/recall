Behavior Cloning

The Behavior Cloning i.i.d. Violation
The core issue
In standard supervised learning we assume train and test data are drawn from the
same distribution. In behavior cloning this is false: the distribution of states the
learner visits depends on the learner’s own policy.
Expert’s training data: states from dπ∗ .

Learner’s test data: states from dπ̂ ,
where π̂ is the learned BC policy.
The learner can end up in states that are
out of distribution w.r.t. training data,
leading to compounding error.

Compounding Errors

The O(T 2 ε) compounding-error bound
Suppose BC policy has a per-step error rate ε on the expert’s state distribution.
Theorem (Ross & Bagnell, 2010)
J(π̂) ≤ J(π ∗ ) + T 2 ε.
This bound is tight: there exist problems that incur quadratic regret

Intuition (“snowball effect”):
▶ At each of T steps there is probability ε of a mistake—possibly taking the agent
off-distribution where it may make mistakes at every remaining timestep.
▶ In the worst case, this results in a cost of 1 at every remaining timestep → total
cost of O(T ) once a mistake is made.

The DAgger Algorithm

DAgger: Dataset Aggregation

Key idea: Repeat: (1) run the current policy to observe states it actually visits, (2) ask
the expert for actions for those states (and optionally, sometimes follow the expert
instead of the current policy), (3) aggregate all data and retrain.

The training distribution gradually shifts to cover the states the learner will
actually encounter, eliminating the distribution mismatch that causes compounding
errors.

The DAgger Algorithm

Connection to online learning: Follow-The-Leader
DAgger is a Follow-The-Leader (FTL) algorithm.
Online learning recap:
▶ At round i we pick policy π̂i
▶ Loss function ℓi is revealed
▶ Our goal: minimise regret vs. best fixed
policy in hindsight
FTL rule: at each round, pick the policy that
minimises the total loss on data seen so far.
This results in a linear regret guarantee with
respect to T .
Limitations of Behavior Cloning

No-regret online learners
Any no-regret* online learner in place
of the “retrain on D” step yields the
same linear regret guarantee.
*no-regret does not mean zero regret,
but only that average regret approaches
zero in the limit

Theoretical Guarantees

The main guarantee (infinite samples per iteration)
P
Let εN = minπ∈Π N1 N
i=1 Es∼dπi [ℓ(s, π)] be the expected per-timestep loss of the best
representable policy in hindsight over all N iterations of data
If N is Õ(uT ), then there exists a returned policy π̂ such that:
J(π̂) ≤ J(π ∗ ) + u T εN + O(1)
where u measures the worst-case cost of a single wrong action.
Caveat: Only true for convex policy classes and losses; otherwise cannot guarantee loss
decreases at each iteration. Nevertheless, tends to work well in practice, e.g., neural nets.
Note: The paper also contains a probabilistic finite-sample guarantee.
